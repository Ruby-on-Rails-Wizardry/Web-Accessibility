WCAG2ICT describes how WCAG 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2 principles, guidelines, and success criteria can be applied to **documents, software, and other ICT** — including mobile apps, native applications, platform software, and software on ICT with closed functionality.

It was first published in 2013 (WCAG 2.0 A + AA) and helped regulators (Section 508, EN 301 549) adopt WCAG for non-web ICT. The current Note covers WCAG 2 Level A and AA.

## Intent

- Provide **replacements for web-based language** in WCAG criteria and definitions (“web page” → “document” / “software”).
- Flag where WCAG assumes a browser, user agent, or assistive technology that may not exist (closed products, some native stacks).

It does **not**:

- decide which criteria should or should not apply to a given product
- change WCAG or its supporting documents
- cover hardware (WCAG does not apply to hardware)
- provide implementation techniques
- describe how non-web ICT should “conform” to WCAG
- incorporate WCAG supplemental / COGA guidance

Fully addressing non-web accessibility involves requirements **beyond** WCAG and WCAG2ICT.

## Who it is for

Standards organizations, ICT managers familiar with WCAG, developers and designers, policy makers, auditors, educators, vendors.

## Relation to U.S. rules

- **Section 508 E205.4** incorporates WCAG 2.0 A + AA and, for non-web documents, substitutes “document” for “web page.” Four criteria are excepted: 2.4.1, 2.4.5, 3.2.3, 3.2.4. See [../section-508/revised-standards.md](../section-508/revised-standards.md).
- **ADA Title II** applies WCAG 2.1 AA to mobile apps as well as web content. DOJ cites WCAG2ICT-related thinking in the rulemaking record. See [../ada/title-ii-web-mobile.md](../ada/title-ii-web-mobile.md).
