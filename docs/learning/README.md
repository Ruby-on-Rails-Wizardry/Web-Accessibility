# Learning theory (Bloom)

Local reference notes for two works that sit next to this project's accessibility work: how to **name** what a learner should be able to do, and how to **teach until they can**.

These are **not** the official books. The 1956 *Taxonomy* and the 1968 *Learning for Mastery* paper remain under copyright. ERIC's [copyright policy](https://eric.ed.gov/?copyright) says authors and publishers retain rights; ERIC cannot grant republication. What is here is a bibliographic map, public structure, and a fair-use study guide with links to the originals.

## Why they are in this repo

- **Taxonomy** — a vocabulary for outcomes. “Know WCAG 2.2 AA exists” is not the same as “apply 1.3.1 to a form” or “evaluate a page against a profile.” Agents and developers can pitch work at the right level instead of mixing recall with judgment.
- **Learning for Mastery** — Bloom’s claim that most learners (he wrote “perhaps over 90 percent”) can reach a defined standard if time, instruction quality, and correctives vary. That is the same idea as [requirement profiles](../profiles/requirement-profiles.md): name mastery, diagnose, prescribe the next piece, do not sort people on a curve.

Neither paper is an accessibility standard. They do not change WCAG, 508, or ADA.
