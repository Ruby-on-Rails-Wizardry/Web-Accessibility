WCAG groups its work under four principles. Use them as a **map** when you ask why a pattern exists. Do not memorize them as a quiz of letters.

| Letter | If this fails | Someone cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P**erceivable | The information is not available to their senses or tools | Notice that the thing is there |
| **O**perable | They cannot reach or act on it | Use it (keyboard, time, seizures, …) |
| **U**nderstandable | The interface or text does not make sense | Know what to do next |
| **R**obust | Other software cannot talk to it reliably | Use their assistive technology |

A later node (a label, a heading, a focus ring) should name which letter it serves, then walk back to [comparable access](/learn/comparable-access/) and [equal worth](/learn/equal-worth/).
